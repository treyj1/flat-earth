FOLD A GLOBE

Every flat map stretches the world somewhere. A globe does not. No globe at home? Print one of these maps, cut it out, and fold it back up into a world you can hold in your hand.

THE BUTTERFLY MAP

CAHILL BUTTERFLY  /  8 TRIANGLES

Eight triangles fold into a pointy globe called an octahedron. It comes out about 4 inches across on Letter paper and about 6 inches on Tabloid.

  • Letter paper 8.5 x 11 in, PDF, 1.2 MB

THE DYMAXION MAP

DYMAXION  /  20 TRIANGLES

Twenty triangles fold into a rounder globe called an icosahedron. It comes out about 3 inches across on Letter paper and about 5 inches on Tabloid.

  • Letter paper 8.5 x 11 in, PDF, 1.2 MB
  • Tabloid paper 11 x 17 in, PDF, 2.4 MB
  • The page from the book 12 x 12 in, PDF, 2.1 MB

YOU WILL NEED

  • A printer, and thin cardstock if you have it. Plain paper works too.
  • Scissors and a glue stick.
  • A ruler, to help you fold straight.

MAKE IT

  1. Print at actual size (100%). Turn off “fit to page”.
  2. Cut on the solid lines.
  3. Fold on the dashed lines. Fold every one away from you, so the map stays on the outside.
  4. Glue each flap under the edge with its letter. Save the last flaps for the end.

Tip for grown-ups: the last few points are fiddly. A small piece of tape holds the shape while the glue dries.

MAKE A STAR WHEEL

A spinning map of the sky over State College. Turn it to any date and time, and its window shows the stars over your head right then. It is made for 41 degrees north, so it works from Virginia to New York.

PRINT AT HOME

The pieces in the book are 12 inches wide, too big for most printers. These packs shrink all three pages by the same amount, so they fit. Pick the paper you have.

  • Letter paper pack 8.5 x 11 in, 3 pages, PDF, 5.3 MB The wheel comes out about 7 1/2 inches across.
  • Tabloid paper pack 11 x 17 in, 3 pages, PDF, 5.3 MB The wheel comes out about 10 inches across.

THE BOOK PAGES

Full size, 12 x 12 inches, the same as the book. For a big printer or a print shop. Print both pieces at the same size.

  • How to make it 12 x 12 in, PDF, 1.7 MB
  • Piece 1: star disk 12 x 12 in, PDF, 2.0 MB
  • Piece 2: front cover 12 x 12 in, PDF, 1.5 MB

YOU WILL NEED

  • A printer.
  • Thin cardboard, like a cereal box, and a glue stick. Or print on cardstock.
  • Scissors and a sharp pencil.
  • One brass paper fastener.
  • A red flashlight, for reading it outside at night.

MAKE IT

  1. Print at actual size (100%). Both pieces must come out the same size.
  2. Glue each piece onto thin cardboard and let it dry flat. Printed on cardstock? Skip this step.
  3. Cut out the star disk along its dashed circle. Cut out the cover, then cut out its window. Keep the thin bridge and the round pad in the middle.
  4. Poke a small hole through the red cross on each piece with the pencil.
  5. Stack the cover on top of the disk, stars facing up. Push the brass fastener through both holes and open its legs on the back. Leave it loose enough to turn.

USE IT

  1. Turn the disk until today’s date lines up with the time. Use the black times in winter and the red times during daylight saving time.
  2. Face north, south, east or west. Hold the wheel over your head with that word at the bottom, nearest you.
  3. The window is your sky. Its edge is the horizon. The middle, just below the bridge, is the spot straight overhead.
Drawn in crayon by code, same as the book. Map data: Natural Earth.
